Gray Whale Population Estimate
Eastern Pacific population, 1994 2025 Scroll down to watch the story unfold
1994 Removed from Endangered Species List
Gray whale population estimated at ~21,000 After decades of protection from commercial whaling, the species is declared fully recovered a landmark conservation victory.
2016 Population Peak
Numbers reach an estimated 27,000 whales Whale watching thrives along the California Central Coast, including in San Luis Obispo. Researchers consider the gray whale a model conservation success.
2019-2020 NOAA Unusual Mortality Event
NOAA declares an Unusual Mortality Event as over 700 strandings are recorded along the Pacific Coast Scientists link the die-off to shrinking Arctic food supplies driven by climate change.
2023 Nearly 50% Lost
Population estimated at ~14,500 - a near 50% drop from the 2016 peak Calf counts fall to historic lows. Whale watching businesses along the Central Coast report a near-total collapse in gray whale sightings.
2025 San Francisco Bay Crisis
A record 21 gray whales strand in San Francisco Bay in a single season, likely due to starvation and exhaustion. Researchers including Dr. Ted Cheeseman call for the species to be relisted as endangered.
